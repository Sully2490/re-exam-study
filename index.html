FL Sales Associate Cheat Sheet (verified 10/14/2026)

Sources: DBPR CIB "Effective January 2025" (RE_sales_cibs.pdf); F.S. text at flsenate.gov (2025 ed.); 61J2 F.A.C. text via Cornell LII (mirror of the official FAC; flrules.org blocked the automated fetch).

Exam facts (CIB)

  • 100 multiple-choice questions, 3.5 hours, closed book. 75 or higher passes. Pilot questions may be added; they are unscored and come with extra time.
  • Silent, hand-held, battery calculator allowed (financial calculators OK).

Escrow (highest-yield law)

  • Sales associate → broker: end of next business day (61J2-14.009).
  • Broker deposits "immediately" = end of 3rd business day after receipt; Saturdays, Sundays, and holidays excluded (61J2-14.008(3)).
  • Title company or attorney holds the deposit: the broker requests written verification within 10 business days of the due date, then reports to the seller's broker within 10 business days (61J2-14.008(2)(b)).
  • Conflicting demands or good-faith doubt: notify FREC in writing within 15 business days; start a settlement procedure within 30 business days (61J2-10.032). The four procedures are an escrow disbursement order (EDO), mediation, arbitration, and litigation (F.S. 475.25(1)(d)1).
  • Broker funds allowed in escrow: $1,000 per sales account, $5,000 per property-management account (61J2-14.010(2)).
  • Monthly written reconciliation, signed and dated by the broker (61J2-14.012(2)). Reasonable time to correct escrow errors with no shortage = 30 days from the last reconciliation (61J2-14.010(2)).

Brokerage relationships (F.S. 475.278)

  • Presumption: transaction broker unless single agent or no brokerage relationship is established in writing.
  • TB duties: deal honestly and fairly; account for funds; skill, care, diligence; disclose known facts materially affecting value (residential, not readily observable); present all offers timely; limited confidentiality; other duties agreed in writing. No loyalty, no full confidentiality.
  • Single agent adds loyalty, confidentiality, obedience, and full disclosure. The written disclosure goes in a separate document or the listing/representation agreement.
  • Dual agency is not allowed. Single agent → TB requires a transition consent in writing (CIB IV.B.4).
  • Do the disclosures apply? They cover residential sales (≤4 units, unimproved residential ≤10 acres). Unverified here: exact scope wording; check 475.278(4) / 475.01.

Licensing

  • Pre-license: max 63 hours (475.17(2)). Post-license: 45 hours before 1st renewal (475.17(3)(a); 61J2-3.020). After that, 14 hours CE each renewal (61J2-3.009).
  • Voluntary inactive: renewable with CE (≤12 hrs/yr inactive). Involuntarily inactive > 2 years = expires (475.183).
  • Mailing or email address change: notify within 10 days (61J2-10.038).
  • Unlicensed practice: 3rd-degree felony (475.42(1)(a)).
  • Recovery fund: $50,000 per transaction / $150,000 aggregate per licensee (475.484).
  • Ads: must include the licensed brokerage name. If an individual is named, at least the last name as registered. Online, the firm name goes adjacent to the contact info (61J2-10.025).

Property / taxes

  • Homestead: $25,000 off all levies, plus an additional up to $25,000 on value over $50,000, non-school levies only, CPI-indexed each Jan 1. The qualification date is Jan 1 (F.S. 196.031). The current indexed dollar amount is UNVERIFIED (it changes yearly); the exam most likely uses $25k + $25k.
  • Tenancy by entireties: spouses only; survivorship; unity of marriage. TIC: no survivorship. JTWROS: four unities (time, title, interest, possession).
  • Landlord-tenant (F.S. 83.49): return the deposit in 15 days if no claim; 30 days to send written notice of a claim; written disclosure within 30 days of receipt.
  • Florida is a lien-theory state (general knowledge; not re-verified today).

Contracts and mortgages

  • Void (no contract) vs. voidable (minor, fraud, duress) vs. unenforceable.
  • Option: the optionee holds the right; the optionor is bound.
  • "Subject to": the buyer has no personal liability. Assumption: the buyer is liable. Novation: the seller is released.
  • Clauses: acceleration (lender calls the whole loan due on default), alienation/due-on-sale, defeasance (lender must satisfy the mortgage when paid), prepayment.

Common traps

  1. Associate (next business day) vs. broker (3rd business day) deposit deadlines.
  2. Business days exclude weekends and holidays.
  3. LTV uses the lower of price or appraisal.
  4. Points are on the loan, not the price.
  5. Proration: identify who owns closing day and whether the item is paid in arrears or in advance.
  6. Doc stamps: round any fraction of $100 up. The note is $.35 and the deed is $.70; don't swap them.
  7. Homestead's second $25k does not apply to school taxes or the $25k–$50k band.
  8. Section fractions: multiply all of them.
  9. In the cost approach, never depreciate land.

D1: $4,380.00 debit seller / credit buyer. Daily rate 5,475/365 = $15.00. Seller days Jan 1–Oct 19 = 292. 292 × 15 = $4,380.00. Unpaid (paid in arrears), so debit seller, credit buyer.

D2: $900 debit seller / credit buyer. Daily rate 1,800/30 = $60. The buyer owns Sep 16–30 = 15 days × 60 = $900. The seller holds the buyer's rent: debit seller, credit buyer.

D3: $591.78. Annual interest = 180,000 × .06 = 10,800; daily = 10,800/365 = 29.589. Seller owes Sep 1–20 = 20 days → $591.78. Debit seller, credit buyer.

D4: $9,961.88. 575,000 × .055 = 31,625.00; × .45 = 14,231.25; × .70 = 9,961.88
